Change Begins With Us

Understanding social change and development helps us see why communities face challenges, how people create change, and how social work can help build a fairer and more inclusive society.

Behind every social issue is a person, a family, and a story worth listening to. Real change begins when we choose to understand, care, and stand with one another.

Start with the important ideas

What Do We Mean by Change and Development?

Social change and development are connected, but they do not mean exactly the same thing. Both involve people, institutions, opportunities, and the conditions that shape everyday life.

SOCIAL CHANGE

Society does not stay the same

Social change refers to changes in social relationships, values, behavior, culture, institutions, and ways of living. It may happen slowly or quickly, and it can bring positive or negative effects.

DEVELOPMENT

Improving people's lives

Development is a process of improving people's quality of life and expanding opportunities in education, health, livelihood, safety, freedom, and participation—not simply increasing income.

SOCIAL PROGRESS

No one should be left behind

Meaningful progress happens when people can participate, access services, exercise their rights, and help make decisions that affect their families and communities.

Look beyond individual situations

People's Lives Are Connected.

A family's situation is affected not only by personal choices, but also by access to jobs, education, health services, government support, culture, and the environment. These conditions are connected.

For example, when a parent loses a job, the family's income may decrease. This can affect food, children's schooling, health, and relationships. Understanding these connections helps us avoid blaming individuals for problems that are also shaped by wider social conditions.

“Social change becomes meaningful when improvements in society are felt in people's everyday lives.”

Functionalism

Society is made up of connected parts—such as family, education, government, and the economy—that perform important functions. When these parts work together, they can contribute to social stability. When one part struggles, other parts may also be affected.

Community example: Schools, families, local government, and health centers need to coordinate so children can learn, stay healthy, and receive support.

Development is influenced by several connected factors. A problem in one area can affect other parts of people's lives.

Real people, real contributions

Social Change in Action

Public figures can help bring attention to social issues through journalism, sports, music, volunteer work, and advocacy. These examples show how individual actions can support wider social change—but lasting progress also requires communities, institutions, and public policies.

Journalism · Freedom of expression

Maria Ressa

Filipino journalist and co-founder of Rappler. She received the 2021 Nobel Peace Prize with Dmitry Muratov for efforts to safeguard freedom of expression.

Connection: Conflict Theory and social justice. Her work highlights the importance of accountability, access to information, and the role of a free press in a democracy.
Sports · Women’s empowerment

Hidilyn Diaz

She won the Philippines’ first-ever Olympic gold medal at the Tokyo 2020 Games, competing in women’s weightlifting.

Connection: Social change and gender equality. Her achievement challenged stereotypes about women in sports and inspired wider recognition of Filipino women athletes.
Music · Children’s rights

Gary Valenciano

The singer has served as a UNICEF Philippines National Ambassador and has used his public platform to raise awareness about children’s rights and education.

Connection: Human development. His advocacy draws attention to children’s well-being, learning, and the opportunities they need to develop.
Film · Children’s rights

Anne Curtis

The actress and television host has worked with UNICEF Philippines as a National Ambassador, including advocacy related to children’s immunization and early childhood education.

Connection: Human development and people-centered development. Healthy childhood and early learning help expand a child’s future opportunities.
Volunteerism · Disaster response

Angel Locsin

The Philippine Red Cross has documented her participation in relief packing and distribution activities, including support for families affected by disasters.

Connection: Community solidarity and social work values. Volunteer action can support affected families, while organized disaster response and public services remain essential.

Remember: These people are examples of public advocacy and contribution—not proof that one person alone can solve a social problem. Social change is stronger when people work together and systems also improve.

Connecting theories to everyday Filipino life

What Is Happening in the Philippines Today?

Social change is not just an idea in a textbook. We can see it in the daily experiences of Filipino families, workers, students, and communities. These examples use recent reports available as of November 2026.

Poverty · Food security

1. Rising pressure on household budgets

55%

In its September 2026 survey, Social Weather Stations reported that 55% of Filipino families rated themselves as poor. Self-rated food poverty also rose to 44%.

Why it matters: Families may need to choose between food, transportation, school needs, medicine, and bills. Social workers can help connect households to appropriate services and support, while advocating for decent work and responsive social protection.
Source: SWS, October 13, 2026. Self-rated survey results reflect how respondents describe their situation; they are not the same as the official poverty rate.
Employment · Economic security

2. The challenge of finding secure work

Having a job does not always mean having stable income, benefits, or protection. The World Bank's 2026 report notes that only about one in three Filipino workers contributes to social security.

Why it matters: Informal and insecure work can make families vulnerable when illness, job loss, or old age happens. Social development includes decent work, fair opportunities, and access to social protection.
Source: World Bank, Building the Filipino Middle Class, June 4, 2026.
Climate change · Disaster risk

3. Floods, typhoons, and vulnerable communities

The Philippines remains highly exposed to extreme weather. UN reporting noted that 23 tropical cyclones affected the country in 2025, while flooding and landslides affected millions.

Why it matters: Low-income families, older persons, children, persons with disabilities, and people in hazard-prone areas may face greater risks. Social work includes preparedness, evacuation support, relief coordination, psychosocial support, and helping communities recover.
Source: United Nations Philippines, 2025 annual results and 2026 Joint Work Plan.
Education · Equal opportunity

4. Unequal access to quality learning

Students do not all have the same learning conditions. Some have reliable internet, devices, books, and quiet study spaces; others share gadgets, travel far, or help support their families.

Why it matters: The digital divide and unequal learning resources can widen existing gaps. A people-centered response includes accessible learning support, school-community partnerships, and services for children at risk of falling behind.
Context: OECD Economic Surveys: Philippines 2026 identifies the quality and reach of public services, including education, as important development concerns.
Cost of living · Food and transport

5. Higher costs affect everyday choices

When fuel, transport, and food costs rise, low-income households feel the pressure quickly because a large share of their budget goes to basic needs. A 2026 UN Philippines report described how global fuel-price shocks affected domestic prices earlier in the year.

Why it matters: Economic changes can affect nutrition, school attendance, access to health care, and family well-being. Social workers should understand these wider pressures when assessing a family's needs.
Source: United Nations Philippines, 2026 Joint Work Plan summary.
Technology · Digital inclusion

6. Digital access: opportunity and exclusion

Online learning, job applications, banking, and government services can save time—but people without a device, stable connection, digital skills, or accessible platforms may be left behind.

Why it matters: Technology can support modernization, but development must be inclusive. Digital services should be paired with face-to-face assistance and accessible alternatives.
Related perspective: Modernization, balanced with the Human Development Approach.
From understanding to meaningful action

How Social Work Contributes

Social work connects people with resources, strengthens relationships, supports communities, and addresses barriers that prevent people from participating fully in society.

Empowerment and participation

Social workers help people recognize their strengths, express their needs, participate in decisions, and become active partners in finding solutions. People should not be treated only as recipients of help.

Human rights and social justice

Social work promotes dignity, equality, inclusion, and access to services. It also challenges discrimination and unfair systems that limit people's opportunities.

Community organizing and partnership

Social workers work with families, community members, local leaders, government agencies, and organizations. Cooperation helps make programs more responsive and sustainable.

People-centered and holistic practice

Look at the whole situation of a person or family. Consider personal strengths, family relationships, community resources, and wider social conditions before planning support.

Example from community development practice

In community-based work such as KALAHI-CIDSS, residents identify their needs, take part in planning, and work together on community priorities. This reflects participation and empowerment: solutions are stronger when community members have a voice and ownership.
